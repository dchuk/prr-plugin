# Glossary of Roadmapping Terminology

Every term below appears in *Product Roadmaps Relaunched*. Brief definitions are given here; follow the cross-references for full context and usage guidance.

---

## Problem Framing

Roadmapping conversations collapse when participants use the same word to mean different things. "Roadmap" means a Gantt chart to one executive and a living strategic statement to a product manager; "theme" means a color scheme to a designer and a customer-need cluster to a PM. This glossary fixes shared language so every cross-functional conversation starts from the same definitions.

---

## Terms A–Z

### Alignment
A concerted effort to help people understand the issues and their respective roles, involving asking questions and listening to feedback from internal and external stakeholders. People with differing opinions can still align on their intentions. Alignment is not consensus.

_Cross-reference: [alignment-and-buyin.md](../patterns/alignment-and-buyin.md)_

---

### Approach A
The course-correction strategy for organizations with a score of **12 or higher** on the Roadmap Health Assessment; involves prioritizing and improving one part of the roadmapping process at a time. _source: ch14_

_Cross-reference: [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md)_

---

### Approach B
The full relaunch strategy for organizations with a Roadmap Health Assessment score of **11 or lower**; involves starting from scratch by following the book's steps in order, often through a roadmap workshop. _source: ch14_

_Cross-reference: [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md)_

---

### Carryover Items
Roadmap items that were not completed in a given period and are pushed from one roadmap version to the next as a result of delays in delivery. _source: ch13_

_Cross-reference: [keeping-roadmap-fresh.md](keeping-roadmap-fresh.md)_

---

### Collaboration
Individuals cooperating to accomplish a common goal or outcome, working together for a shared purpose even if they don't agree on every step along the way. Distinct from both alignment and consensus.

_Cross-reference: [alignment-and-buyin.md](../patterns/alignment-and-buyin.md)_

---

### Complementary Information
Information not formally part of a product roadmap (project schedules, platform considerations, financials, external drivers) that may provide helpful context for stakeholders in joint presentations. _source: ch05, ch12_

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-components.md](../core/roadmap-components.md)_

---

### Confidence Percentage
A multiplier applied to the Value/Effort result in the ROI Scorecard to discount for risk and unknowns; the inverse of risk. Less confident proposals receive a lower multiplier. Also used on roadmap items to communicate to stakeholders how likely delivery is as planned. _source: ch10, ch12_

_Cross-reference: [prioritization-frameworks.md](../patterns/prioritization-frameworks.md)_

---

### Consensus
In practice: hours of discussion leading to decisions everyone supposedly agrees to, but that no individual can be held accountable for, often leading to barriers to implementation. Consensus is neither necessary nor desirable for roadmap decisions.

_Cross-reference: [alignment-and-buyin.md](../patterns/alignment-and-buyin.md)_

---

### Critical Path Journey
The sequence of must-have pain points in the customer's journey, linked together, that forms the blueprint for creating a minimum viable product. One of the five canonical prioritization frameworks. _source: ch10_

_Cross-reference: [prioritization-frameworks.md](../patterns/prioritization-frameworks.md)_

---

### Customer
The recipient of the value your product provides, encompassing both the buyer and the user; the two may be the same person or different people. _source: ch04_

---

### Delighters (Wows)
In the Kano Model, exciting needs that go beyond customer expectations and typically define the offerings of category leaders. May map to MoSCoW Could Haves. _source: ch10_

_Cross-reference: [prioritization-frameworks.md](../patterns/prioritization-frameworks.md)_

---

### Discovery Period
A post-release evaluation phase during which a team learns whether a released product, feature, or process change has had the intended effect; recommended for internal process changes as well as product releases. _source: ch14_

_Cross-reference: [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md)_

---

### Dissatisfiers
In the Kano Model, expected needs that, if not met, will cause customers not to buy or use the product. Equivalent to critical path items and MoSCoW Must Haves. _source: ch10_

_Cross-reference: [prioritization-frameworks.md](../patterns/prioritization-frameworks.md)_

---

### Feature Factory
A product team that releases feature after feature with no tie back to the reason for those features and no connection to intended outcomes. The risk of an output-focused roadmap. _source: ch07_

See **[roadmap-anti-patterns.md](../anti-patterns/roadmap-anti-patterns.md)** for a full treatment of this and related anti-patterns.

---

### Functional Needs
Customer-focused needs that your product must address, as distinguished from technical or nonfunctional needs. _source: ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### GROW
An acronym for structuring shuttle diplomacy conversations: **G**oals, **R**eality, **O**ptions, **W**ay forward. _source: ch11_

_Cross-reference: [alignment-and-buyin.md](../patterns/alignment-and-buyin.md)_

---

### Guardrails and Goalposts
The strategic boundaries and targets within which autonomous engineering and product teams operate; they define the 'what' and 'why' while leaving the 'how' to the team. _source: ch04_

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-definition.md](../core/roadmap-definition.md)_

---

### IKEA Effect
As applied to roadmaps: involving stakeholders in the development and refinement of a product roadmap radically increases their buy-in, because people assign greater value to things they had a hand in creating. _source: ch12_

_Cross-reference: [presenting-and-sharing.md](../patterns/presenting-and-sharing.md)_

---

### Iron Triangle
A model representing the four interdependent levers of any project — schedule, scope, quality, and budget (resources) — that must be consciously traded off when unplanned change occurs. Holding all variables constant when one slips causes quality to degrade silently. _source: ch13_

_Cross-reference: [keeping-roadmap-fresh.md](keeping-roadmap-fresh.md)_

---

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
Metrics that define how product success will be measured before release; the data giving meaningful feedback on how a product is doing. Often the metrics measuring key results from OKRs. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

### Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
As defined by Eric Ries in *The Lean Startup*: "that version of a new product which allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least effort." A deliberate quality trade-off strategy for startups in uncertain markets. _source: ch13_

_Cross-reference: [keeping-roadmap-fresh.md](keeping-roadmap-fresh.md)_

---

### Mission
The intent you hold right now and the purpose driving you to realize your vision. Must reflect what you do for someone else — typically your customers. Not what you value, nor a vision for the future. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

### MoSCoW
A prioritization framework labeling items as **M**ust Have, **S**hould Have, **C**ould Have, and **W**on't Have (this time). Must Haves are minimum-to-ship; Won't Haves are explicitly deferred. One of the five canonical prioritization frameworks. _source: ch10_

_Cross-reference: [prioritization-frameworks.md](../patterns/prioritization-frameworks.md)_

---

### Now / Next / Later
A non-date-based broad timeframe convention for roadmaps that provides sequencing guidance while preserving flexibility. Satisfies stakeholder needs for sequence without creating false precision. _source: ch05, ch07, ch13_

Always written with slashes and capital letters: **Now / Next / Later**. Never substitute "short-term / medium-term / long-term."

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-components.md](../core/roadmap-components.md)_

---

### Objectives and Key Results (OKRs)
A framework where objectives are specific qualitative goals and key results are quantitative measures of progress toward those objectives. First used by Andrew Grove at Intel in the early 1980s. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

### Opportunity-Solution Tree
A visualization (developed by Teresa Torres) that organizes desired outcomes, opportunities (themes), solutions, and experiments hierarchically to simplify decision-making by separating decisions among like things at each level. _source: ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### Osborne Effect
The phenomenon where pre-announcing future products causes current sales to fall sharply, as customers wait for the announced version. Named after the Osborne 1 computer whose sales collapsed in 1983 after founder Adam Osborne pre-announced superior new models. _source: ch12_

_Cross-reference: [presenting-and-sharing.md](../patterns/presenting-and-sharing.md)_

---

### Outcome
The difference that an output makes — e.g., keeping a child safe in the car, as opposed to producing a car seat. "Outcomes are the difference made by the outputs." (Deb Mills-Scofield) Roadmap themes should represent outcomes, not outputs. _source: ch07, ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### Output
The stuff produced — physical or virtual — for a specific type of customer, such as features, products, or services. Distinct from the outcome (the difference it makes). _source: ch07, ch08_

---

### Pivot
A change in strategy without a change in vision, as defined by Eric Ries in *The Lean Startup*. Implemented when a company realizes its current strategy is unlikely to achieve its vision. _source: ch13_

_Cross-reference: [keeping-roadmap-fresh.md](keeping-roadmap-fresh.md)_

---

### Product
How you deliver value to your organization's customers; includes physical artifacts, services, and experiences. _source: ch04_

---

### Product Core
The initial, limited circle of stakeholders for a relaunched roadmap consisting of product people, engineers, and designers most closely involved in the product effort, before gradually expanding to broader stakeholders. _source: ch14_

_Cross-reference: [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md)_

---

### Product Roadmap
A strategic communication tool describing how you intend to achieve your product vision; focuses on value to be delivered to the customer and organization; a statement of intent and direction, not a project plan. _source: ch04_

Full definition: [roadmap-definition.md](../core/roadmap-definition.md)

---

### Product Strategy
The bridge connecting the high-level vision to the specifics of the roadmap. Makes the vision explicit and concrete by explaining how it will be achieved, typically via objectives. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

### Product Vision
Clarifies why a product is being brought to market and what its success will mean to the world and the organization. The raison d'être of the entire effort and the basis of the roadmap. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

### Project Plan
A detailed operational document tracking specific feature delivery and resource use; not to be conflated with the product roadmap, which is a strategic document. _source: ch04_

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-definition.md](../core/roadmap-definition.md)_

---

### Punctuated Equilibrium
Borrowed from evolutionary theory: "a theory that evolution proceeds with long periods of relative stability interspersed with rapid change." Used as a metaphor for managing roadmap change in the least disruptive way — allowing stable execution between deliberate, periodic updates. _source: ch13_

_Cross-reference: [keeping-roadmap-fresh.md](keeping-roadmap-fresh.md)_

---

### Release Plan
A tactical artifact about execution, listing specific delivery dates and features; distinct from the strategic product roadmap and sitting below it in the planning hierarchy. _source: ch04_

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-definition.md](../core/roadmap-definition.md)_

---

### Roadmap Health Assessment
A 14-question scored checklist (0–2 per question, max 22 points) used to evaluate the state of an organization's roadmapping process and determine whether to take Approach A (course corrections) or Approach B (full relaunch). _source: ch14_

_Cross-reference: [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md)_

---

### Roadmap Workshop
A focused multi-day collaborative event modeled on a Design Sprint that brings key stakeholders together to define product vision, objectives, customer needs, and an initial roadmap, then agree on next steps like customer validation and revision cadence. _source: ch14_

_Cross-reference: [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md)_

---

### Satisfiers
In the Kano Model, normal needs that customers do not strictly require but that will satisfy them when met. Roughly equivalent to MoSCoW Should Haves. _source: ch10_

_Cross-reference: [prioritization-frameworks.md](../patterns/prioritization-frameworks.md)_

---

### Shuttle Diplomacy
A tactic borrowed from international negotiation in which the product manager meets with each stakeholder individually rather than as a group, acting as a neutral intermediary to incrementally build agreement. Involves asking questions and listening before a group presentation to prevent surprises. _source: ch11, ch12, ch14_

_Cross-reference: [alignment-and-buyin.md](../patterns/alignment-and-buyin.md)_

---

### Silos
Isolated, unilateral decision-making or planning done without cross-functional input, which kills enthusiasm and momentum for a product. _source: ch11_

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-anti-patterns.md](../anti-patterns/roadmap-anti-patterns.md)_

---

### Stage of Development
A secondary roadmap label (e.g., "discovery," "design," "prototyping," "pre-production," "beta") communicating how mature a theme or feature is and how tangible it will be to stakeholders. _source: ch05_

_Cross-reference: [roadmap-components.md](../core/roadmap-components.md)_

---

### Stakeholder
All internal and external colleagues and partners involved with the product being developed, marketed, sold, and serviced. _source: ch04_

---

### Subtheme
A more specific, granular customer need that sits beneath a theme on the roadmap. Themes can stand alone or represent a grouping of subthemes. _source: ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### Technical Debt
The accumulation of quality issues in software — caused by shortcuts, corner-cutting, or working under pressure — that slow future development and can seriously hamper the ability to advance the roadmap. May require rearchitecture or replatforming before new features or users can be added effectively. _source: ch12, ch13_

_Cross-reference: [keeping-roadmap-fresh.md](keeping-roadmap-fresh.md)_

---

### Technical (Nonfunctional) Needs
Engineering-focused system or infrastructure needs required to make the product function, as distinguished from customer-facing functional needs. _source: ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### Theme
An organizational construct for defining what's important to your customers at the present time; a high-level customer need expressed on the product roadmap. Themes focus on outcomes rather than output, and they answer the question: "What would need to be true for our product to realize its vision and attain its business objectives?" _source: ch04, ch05, ch07, ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### Theme Card
A visual artifact representing a single theme on the roadmap, including a color-coded section identifying the business objective(s) the theme relates to. _source: ch08_

_Cross-reference: [themes-and-needs.md](../core/themes-and-needs.md)_

---

### Values
Beliefs and ideals that guide behaviors and shape culture — how people behave when no one is watching. Described as a compass for what is right or wrong for the business. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

### Vision
A longer-term outcome that has an impact on the lives of the people your product serves and on your organization. A statement that paints a future reality or world. _source: ch07_

_Cross-reference: [vision-strategy-okrs.md](../core/vision-strategy-okrs.md)_

---

## Quick-Reference Code Example

The ROI Scorecard formula illustrates how several glossary terms — confidence percentage, value, and effort — combine into a single prioritization number:

```
ROI Score = (Value / Effort) × Confidence%
```

- **Value**: estimated customer or business value delivered by the theme
- **Effort**: relative size (T-shirt sizing) of work required
- **Confidence%**: a decimal multiplier (e.g., 0.7 for 70% confident) discounting for risk and unknowns

A theme with Value = 8, Effort = 3, and Confidence = 70% scores: `(8 / 3) × 0.70 ≈ 1.87`

_source: ch10_

## When to Use / When NOT to Use

**Use this glossary when:**
- Onboarding a stakeholder unfamiliar with outcome-driven roadmapping language
- Resolving a terminology dispute in a roadmap review meeting
- Checking which reference file covers a term in depth before generating a prompt or command

**Do not rely solely on this glossary when:**
- You need full framework mechanics — follow the cross-reference links for prioritization frameworks, alignment techniques, or relaunch steps
- You are building a roadmap artifact — use the relevant command (`../../../../commands/build-roadmap-from-vision.md`, `../../../../commands/transform-features-to-themes.md`) instead
- You need scored assessment criteria — see [relaunch-process.md](../topics/relaunch-process.md) for the full Roadmap Health Assessment rubric
